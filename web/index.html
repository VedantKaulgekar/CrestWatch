Follow extreme weather through the whole ensemble, then downscale only where it matters.

A pipeline for forecasters at NCMRWF and IMD: it tracks anomalies in every ensemble member, generates 5 km scenario samples for flagged regions, and reports alerts as probabilities with their evidence.

Open forecaster console Read the method
Data status. This build runs on a synthetic ensemble that follows the NEPS-G schema. It verifies the software, not the forecast skill. Every figure below is labelled accordingly and comes from data/results.json.

Method

Three stages, each with a defined input, output and baseline to beat.

STAGE 1

Ensemble tracking

Extreme Forecast Index per grid point, per-member detection and association, aggregation into a track ensemble with strike probabilities and a spread cone.

STAGE 2

Probabilistic 5 km downscaling

Residual model on the flagged crop only. Draws N samples so the output is a distribution, not one sharp map. A residual diffusion model (JAX) is trained and included.

STAGE 3

Graded alerts

Exceedance probabilities become Low, Moderate and Severe zones with a core coordinate, served as GeoJSON, CAP 1.2 and a REST API.

Evidence in this build

Measured on 30 independent synthetic cases (tracking) and 20 held-out synthetic fields (downscaling).

MeasureValueWhat it shows

Limits, stated up front

  • No NCMRWF data was available. Results on real cyclones (Amphan, Fani, Yaas) and a heatwave are the next step, using leave-one-event-out evaluation.
  • The graph neural network tracker is trained (2,100 steps, on 30 synthetic cases). On 15 held-out cases it is roughly level with the classical ensemble tracker: 67.6 km mean error (54.7 km median) against 65.1 km (55.6 km median). It does not yet clearly beat the classical baseline; more training cases would likely help more than more training steps at this point.
  • The diffusion downscaler is trained (3,800 steps, synthetic pairs, with a high-noise fine-tuning stage for extremes). It preserves peak wind well (ratio 1.00) and beats bicubic on CRPS (1.38 vs 1.77), on a toy 2x task.
  • The synthetic ensemble is over-dispersed, so probabilities are under-confident in the reliability diagram. Real ensembles will differ.
  • Alert thresholds are illustrative and must be replaced with IMD-defined values. Outputs are forecaster guidance, not public warnings.
